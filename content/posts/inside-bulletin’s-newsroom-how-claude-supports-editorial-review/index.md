A manual quality review can take a Bulletin editor more than half an hour per article. Editors need to catch language errors, examine wording, and assess whether the parties involved in a story are represented fairly. Sometimes, they also need to consider whether naming a private individual is justified.

[The Swedish online publication](https://bulletin.nu/) uses tools powered by Claude to support this work. They help translate, shorten, and rephrase articles, while also running automated checks against an editorial checklist.

Anadea worked with Bulletin to build the publication’s digital platform. The newsroom’s experience with Claude adds to that story, showing how Bulletin now uses AI to help prepare and review its content.

Read more about the publication’s platform and Anadea’s work in[ our Bulletin case study](https://anadea.info/projects/bulletin).

## Shortening, Translating, and Reworking Copy

Bulletin’s journalist assistant supports several everyday tasks:

* Shortening articles to produce a more concise version.
* Rephrasing passages to offer alternative wording.
* Helping with translation and identifying language errors.
* Following custom prompts so journalists can request specific changes.

Custom prompts give journalists flexibility in how they use the assistant. They can specify what needs attention, then assess the suggested revision against the article’s meaning and intended focus.

## What the Editorial Checklist Covers

Alongside language support, Bulletin uses an automated checklist to review articles for journalistic quality.

1. The tool checks for biased wording and assesses whether the parties involved in a story are represented fairly. It also examines references to private individuals, considering whether naming them or disclosing other personal details is justified by their role in the events described.
2. Each of these checks involves an editorial decision. A concern about wording needs to be weighed against the facts. Naming someone requires consideration of the circumstances and the public interest in disclosing their identity.
3. Automated checks can flag a passage for closer review. Whether it needs to change depends on context, which is why editorial judgment remains essential when working with AI suggestions.

## At Least 300 Articles a Month

According to Bulletin, **at least 300 articles a month** pass through its editorial tools, and all of its reporters use them. These figures were confirmed on the company’s behalf by Tino Sanandaji, Chairman of the Board at Bulletin Media Sverige AB.

The tools are now part of the newsroom’s regular workflow, supporting translation, language editing, and editorial checks as the team prepares articles for publication.

Sanandaji describes the approach in terms of a familiar challenge for journalists: working quickly while maintaining quality.

*“In journalism there is a tradeoff between quality and speed. Using advanced AI tools combined with human judgement articles can be written fast while maintaining and even raising quality and consistency”*

*Tino Sanandaji, Chairman of the Board, Bulletin Media Sverige AB*

## What Else the Bulletin Team Builds with Claude

Bulletin also uses Claude to develop small utilities and integrate them into its website and custom CRM. The team has built these tools itself with Claude’s help.

Examples include:

* A Google AdSense integration.
* A 2026 FIFA World Cup results widget for the sports section.
* TeleCRM, for tracking calls from the customer service team to clients.

In these projects, Claude assists with software development. The team uses it to build the functionality it needs, while the editorial tools use Claude through the API.

For Bulletin, Claude serves two practical roles: helping reporters work on articles and helping the team build software for the publication.
